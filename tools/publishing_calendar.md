# publishing_calendar

## Purpose

Plan serialized releases so momentum survives contact with the real calendar.

A serial dies from gaps, not from bad chapters.

Readers drop a story during an unannounced break far more often than during a weak chapter.

---

## When To Use

Before launching a serialized story.

Before any planned break.

At the start of every month.

When the draft backlog falls below the safety line.

---

## Inputs

| Input | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| Update frequency | Chapters per day or per week |
| Backlog | Finished, un-published chapters |
| Chapter length | Target words per chapter |
| Platform norms | Expected cadence on the target platform |
| Blackout dates | Holidays and personal conflicts |

---

## Safety Line Rule

Maintain a backlog of at least:

3 days of output for a daily-release story.

10 chapters for a weekly-release story.

If the backlog drops below the line:

Suspend new drafting and rebuild the buffer.

Never let the buffer hit zero.

## Cadence Strategy

### Launch

Release the first chapters together.

Three chapters on day one gives the reader a reason to commit.

### Steady State

Keep the published rhythm constant.

Readers build a habit faster than they build loyalty.

### Before A Break

Announce the break in advance.

End the last chapter before the break on a strong hook, never on a resolution.

### After A Break

Return with a payoff, not a recap.

The first chapter back decides how many readers return.

## Risk Table

| Risk | Signal | Response |
|------|--------|----------|
| Backlog drain | Line crossed | Stop drafting new arcs, finish existing ones |
| Hook fatigue | Same hook type repeats | Rotate via tools/hook_generator.md |
| Rhythm break | Cadence changes without notice | Announce, then restore the old rhythm |
| Reader drift | Completion drop after a gap | Return with a major payoff |
