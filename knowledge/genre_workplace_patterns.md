# Workplace Genre Patterns

Purpose: write workplace and career fiction where the industry, the power structure and the reversal all feel earned, for a reader who has held a job.

Use together with the engine's own pack: genre_urban_supernatural_patterns.md (system/panel workplace), genre_era_crossover_patterns.md (era business/workplace crossover).

---

## Audience

Primary Readers:

* Female 22-45 (core), male 22-45 (secondary)
* Readers who have been through an office, a project, or a bad boss and recognize the politics
* Readers chasing the "underestimated professional proves everyone wrong" wish

---

## Core Reader Expectations

### What The Workplace Reader Wants

* A protagonist who is good at one real, nameable thing, and wins by being good at it
* A power structure the reader recognizes: the boss, the rival, the ally, the client, the HR
* A reversal that is deserved — the schemer is beaten on evidence, not on luck
* Growth that is visible in title, salary, and respect, not just in feelings

### What Breaks The Illusion

* A protagonist who is never actually wrong, just "misunderstood"
* A rival who schemes loudly and never accounts for paper trails
* Industry terms used as decoration instead of as the actual work
* A promotion or funding that appears without a transaction behind it

## Industry Credibility

### Fix The Industry Before Chapter 1

* Pick one real industry and learn its actual vocabulary, workflow, and unit economics
* Every chapter's work must survive a practitioner's skim: the report, the bid, the surgery, the audit
* Use the industry's real terms in dialogue, and never explain them to the reader

### The Transaction Test

Every win must have a visible transaction: who paid, who signed, who approved, who lost. A promotion, a contract, or a funding round that appears without a counterparty reads as fantasy.

---

## Power And Stakeholders

### The Power Map

* A boss whose interests are not the protagonist's
* A rival who competes for the same scarce resource
* An ally with their own ceiling and their own price
* A client, investor, or regulator who can end everything

### The Reversal Economy

* The schemer leaves evidence: emails, records, a paper trail
* The protagonist wins by assembling facts and timing, not by a last-minute coincidence
* One loss per arc where the protagonist is genuinely outplayed or outranked

## Golden Finger (金手指) Limits

| Advantage | Allowed Use | Required Cost |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Rebirth | Knows a trend, a deal, a person's trajectory | Cannot prove the source; must rebuild trust |
| System / panel | Task rewards, skill visibility | Rewards still need a transaction to land |
| Professional skill | Elite competence in one domain | Blind spots outside it; a bad habit to fix |
| Network / background | An open door | A debt or an enemy attached |

The golden finger opens a door. It never removes the office, the paper trail, or the person who can say no.

## Genre Rules

### Competence Over Luck

* The protagonist's wins are explained by skill, evidence, or timing — never by a random discovery
* Display the core competence inside the first three chapters, on a real task
* One meaningful professional mistake per arc, with a visible consequence

### Stakes Escalation

Escalate in layers: a task → a position → a division → a company → a market. Never skip a layer without paying the one below it.

### Failure Modes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Decorative jargon | Terms with no real work | Learn the workflow; show the actual task |
| Lucky hero | Wins by coincidence | Make every win a transaction with a counterparty |
| Loud villain | Schemer leaves no trail | Give the schemer a paper trail and a motive |
| Fantasy promotion | Title jumps without a transaction | Add the signer, the cost, and the loser |
| Romance creep | Romance buries the career | Keep one work scene per chapter minimum |

---

## Reader Retention Factors

* Core competence shown inside the first three chapters
* A reversal the protagonist earns through facts and timing
* A boss and a rival whose interests are concrete and opposed
* One visible milestone (title, salary, funding) per arc
* A named professional cost the protagonist pays per arc

## Chapter Hook Patterns

* 她交上去的方案，正是对手剽窃的那份——而原始版本号还藏在她自己的电脑里
* 会议开到一半，甲方把合同推了回来，点名要他当场重算那笔账
* 他空降的那天，全部门都等着看笑话，却不知道这套流程就是他一手设计的
* 猎头开出的年薪翻了三倍，而现任老板刚把裁员名单放进了他的抽屉
* 审计进场的前一晚，财务总监把一箱凭证搬进了他的办公室

---

## Pacing Notes

* Alternate a work chapter with a personal-life chapter; never two pure-office chapters in a row
* Build a reversal: suspicion → evidence → timing → confrontation → fallout
* Save the company- or market-level payoff for an arc finale; let each milestone change standing
* One emotional payoff per volume: family, friendship, or a relationship resolved

---

## Success Checklist

* The industry's terms and workflow survive a practitioner's skim
* Every win has a visible transaction with a counterparty
* Core competence shown inside the first three chapters
* The schemer leaves a paper trail and the protagonist wins on evidence
* Stakes escalate one layer at a time
* The golden finger opens a door, never removes the office
* One real professional mistake per arc
