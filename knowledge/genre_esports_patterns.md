# Esports Genre Patterns

Purpose: write competitive gaming fiction where the match logic, the meta and the pro ecosystem all hold up for a reader who plays the game.

Use together with genre_game_patterns.md (the in-game system/panel layer) and genre_sports_patterns.md (the match/reporting rhythm it shares).

---

## Audience

Primary Readers:

* Male 15-35 (core), female 15-30 (secondary)
* Players who know the meta and will fact-check the draft, the patch and the pick
* Readers chasing the "nobody from a small-town internet café takes the world title" wish

---

## Core Reader Expectations

### What The Esports Reader Wants

* A protagonist who is provably better at the game, demonstrated through real mechanics, not through "godlike hands"
* A team that forms, fractures and improves; an org with money, coaches and management
* A meta that changes — patches, bans, counter-picks — so no advantage stays safe
* Live-match tension and post-match fallout, including the chat, the interviews and the hot-search

### What Breaks The Illusion

* A match resolved by "he just outplayed everyone" with no described decision
* A golden finger that ignores the patch, the draft or the opponent
* A team of one: no coach, no substitute, no org politics
* The game itself contradicting its own rules between chapters

## The Game Engine

### Fix The Game Before Chapter 1

Design or borrow a game that survives ten volumes:

* Core win condition and match length (one map, best-of-three, a season)
* A handful of roles or positions with distinct jobs
* A draft / ban phase if it is a competitive title
* One or two signature mechanics the protagonist can master and the reader can track

Write the rules down. Every match must be re-playable from them.

### Meta, Patch, Ban

The meta is this genre's clock:

* Introduce a patch or balance change every major arc to force adaptation
* A comfort pick must stop working at least once, so the protagonist has to learn
* Ban and counter-pick create visible mind-games before the match even starts

A protagonist who ignores the meta is not dominant; they are lucky.

---

## Pro Ecosystem

### Team And Org

* A coach whose decisions are not always right, a manager with a budget, a substitute with ambition
* Contracts, transfer windows, and a sponsor that wants results this quarter
* The protagonist's rise changes the org around them, and vice versa

### Fandom And Public

* The chat, the hot-search, the interview and the post-match thread are part of the score
* Black-powder (黑粉) and bandwagon fans create pressure that reaches into the match
* One public mistake per arc, with consequences the protagonist cannot dodge

---

## Golden Finger (金手指) Limits

| Advantage | Allowed Use | Required Cost |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Rebirth | Knows a patch, a pick, a player's ceiling | The meta still moves; the seat must be earned |
| System / training panel | Structured practice, stat visibility | Cannot replace live decision-making |
| Godlike hands | Higher mechanical ceiling | Weak macro, tilts, or a bad habit to fix |
| Tactical mind | Reads the map and the draft | Needs four teammates to execute |

The golden finger raises the ceiling. It never removes the patch, the opponent, or the teammate who fumbles.

## Genre Rules

### Show The Play, Not The Label

* Every decisive moment is a decision the reader can follow: the read, the bait, the flank, the commit
* Replace "他神乎其技地赢下这局" with the actual sequence of moves
* The losing play must be as legible as the winning one

### Competence Cadence

* Signature mechanic shown inside the first three chapters, in a real or trial match
* One full, legible match every 5-8 chapters
* One genuine loss or tilt per arc, against a named opponent or a changed meta

### Failure Modes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Label over play | "神操作" with no sequence | Rewrite as a described decision |
| Patch-proof hero | Meta never hurts him | Rotate the meta; force a new pick |
| One-man team | No coach, no org, no sub | Add org friction and a real teammate |
| Inconsistent game | Rules shift between chapters | Freeze the rules; audit every match |
| Fandom noise only | Chat replaces stakes | Keep the match as the primary engine |

---

## Reader Retention Factors

* Signature mechanic shown inside the first three chapters
* One legible, high-stakes match every 5-8 chapters
* A meta shift that dethrones the protagonist at least once per arc
* A team with real friction and a rival who improves on-screen
* Public pressure that reaches into the match, not just around it

---

## Chapter Hook Patterns

* 生死局的最后一秒，他锁下了那个被版本削成废物的英雄——对面全队笑了
* 夺冠庆功宴的直播里，弹幕刷满了他"代打"的实锤截图
* 教练把他按在替补席上，却不知道对面战队的战术手册就是他写的
* 手机弹出转会合同的那一刻，他正在和四个队友抢最后一个出线名额
* 版本更新删掉的，恰好是他唯一会玩的角色

---

## Pacing Notes

* Alternate practice/scrim chapters with official matches; never two pure-training chapters in a row
* Build a match: stakes → draft → early setback → clutch → fallout
* Save the world-title payoff for a season finale; let each match change standing or relationships
* One non-game emotional payoff per volume: team, family, or a rivalry resolved

---

## Success Checklist

* The game's rules are fixed and every match is re-playable from them
* Every decisive play is a described decision, not a label
* One legible match with a turning point every 5-8 chapters
* The meta moves and forces the protagonist to adapt at least once per arc
* The org and the fandom reach into the match
* The golden finger raises the ceiling, never removes the opponent
* At least one real loss or tilt per arc
