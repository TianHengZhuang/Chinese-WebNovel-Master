# Era Crossover Genre Patterns

Purpose: combine an era base (knowledge/genre_era_patterns.md) with a second genre engine without breaking period credibility.

Use together with the engine's own pack: genre_detective_patterns.md, genre_suspense_patterns.md, genre_urban_supernatural_patterns.md.

---

## Audience

Primary Readers:

* Female 25-45 and male 25-50
* Era readers returning for a favourite engine (mystery, food, medicine, trade)
* Readers burned by "era set dressing" that ignores period constraints

---

## Core Reader Expectations

### What The Crossover Reader Wants

* An engine they already like, delivered without era violations
* Era constraints that make the win feel earned
* One "I wish I could live that again" moment per volume
* A period voice; no modern narrator explaining the era from outside

### What Breaks The Illusion

* A win that ignores supply, authority, information and money at once
* Modern vocabulary in narration
* Era detail that never touches the plot

---

## The Two-Layer Rule

Every crossover has exactly two layers:

| Layer | Role | Owns |
|-------|------|------|
| Era base | Constraint and texture | Prices, objects, policy, language, social rules |
| Engine | Momentum and payoff | The case, the kitchen, the clinic, the ledger |

The era layer constrains the engine. It never decorates it.

Test: delete the era details. If the plot does not change, the crossover is fake.

## Engine Rules

### Era Escrow

The era layer must tax every win. Choose at least two:

* Supply constraint: materials,票证, fuel, transport
* Authority constraint: review, approval, gossip, 举报
* Information constraint: no internet, phone only, word of mouth
* Money constraint: savings, debt, no credit

An engine payoff that bypasses all four reads as fantasy and loses the era audience.

### First Payoff Placement

* Display engine competence inside the first 10 chapters, and tie it to an era-only problem
* The first win must be small: a meal served, a case closed, a fever broken
* Save the structural win (the shop, the unit, the case that names the villain) for the end of the first arc

### Advantage (金手指) Under Era Constraint

| Advantage | Era-Compatible Use | Required Cost |
|-----------|--------------------|---------------|
| Rebirth memory | Knows a trend, not a formula | Cannot explain the source |
| Modern skill | Applies method to local materials | Needs scarce inputs |
| Space / inventory | Stores what the era lacks | Volume limited; secrecy risk |
| System | Trades effort for capability | Costs visible to family |

The advantage moves the story forward. The era decides how far.

---

## Texture Budget

Per chapter:

* Two to three era anchors: one object, one price or wage, one social rule
* One paragraph of period exposition at most; anchor through use, not explanation
* Engine vocabulary stays in the protagonist's mouth, never in narration

Balance check: if a chapter contains no era anchor, add one. If it contains more than four, cut two.

---

## Language Rules

* No vocabulary that post-dates the era (网络, 心理, 压力管理, 项目, 团队)
* Use the period term, not the modern synonym: 供销社, 粮站, 大队, 街道办
* Dialect flavour through word choice and rhythm; do not transcribe phonetics
* Avoid political framing as a plot device; keep family and work at the centre
* Modern values may appear only as conflict, never as narration

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Anachronism | Modern objects, terms or manners appear | Audit every noun against the era coordinate |
| Free-win prophecy | The protagonist is never wrong | Add partial knowledge and one failed bet per arc |
| Engine erasure | The engine solves everything; scarcity gone | Re-apply era escrow to the next payoff |
| Vocabulary drift | Narration slips into modern register | Keep modern terms inside dialogue only |
| Texture dump | Paragraphs of period explanation | Convert into use, price or argument |

---

## Reader Retention Factors

* Engine competence demonstrated inside the first 10 chapters, small-scale first
* One era anchor per chapter at minimum, shown in use rather than explained
* One taxed win per arc, with the tax visible to family or colleagues
* Payoff alternation: never two era-life chapters or two engine chapters in a row
* A named cost the protagonist accepts by the end of each volume

## Chapter Hook Patterns

* 她端出的那碗汤，用的是还没上市的新配方，而隔壁国营饭店的经理就坐在对面
* 案卷上的死亡时间，和公社广播里报的完全对不上
* 他用三十年后才有的记账法盘清了队里的烂账，会计连夜烧了账本
* 电还没通，他的作坊里却亮起了灯——全村的窗户都在看

---

## Pacing Notes

* Alternate an era-life chapter with an engine chapter; never two of the same in a row
* Material payoffs every 3-4 chapters; engine payoffs every arc
* Policy milestones become pivots, not rewards: a new rule closes one door and opens another
* One emotional payoff per volume: reconciliation, marriage, reunion, or a named fear faced

---

## Success Checklist

* Deleting era details would change the plot
* Every engine win is taxed by at least two era constraints
* Engine competence shown inside the first 10 chapters, small-scale first
* No vocabulary that post-dates the era in narration
* Two to three era anchors per chapter, one exposition paragraph at most
* The advantage never removes the era's scarcity
* One emotional payoff per volume
